## Overview

Noodle is a workshop robot arm, in the spirit of the arms that hover around a bench handing you
things. The arm is the goal, but it is also the excuse: it is the one project that forces every
layer of robotics at once. Perception, control, embedded firmware, custom electronics, and the
mechanical design holding it all together.

The name is the honest part. A new arm is a noodle: wobbly, imprecise, overshooting everything.
Getting it to hold still is the whole project.

## Approach

Hardware costs money, so this starts as software. Everything that can be learned and validated in
simulation happens there first, and physical parts only get bought once the thing they are meant to
run is already working.

- **Simulation first**: NVIDIA Isaac Sim and Isaac Lab for the arm and its environment, with
  Omniverse as the scene and asset layer.
- **Papers before parts**: working through the SLAM and perception literature rather than wiring an
  approach together and hoping.
- **Survey the field**: studying what existing arms and robotics companies actually do, so the
  design borrows from solved problems instead of rediscovering them.

## Focus Areas

- **SLAM**: the top priority. Simultaneous localization and mapping, so the arm builds a map of an
  unknown workspace while tracking where it sits inside that map. Visual SLAM specifically, meaning
  pose estimation and mapping from camera input, with that pipeline running on constrained onboard
  compute rather than a workstation.
- **Perception**: turning camera input into objects the arm can reason about and reach for.
- **Embedded**: the firmware and real-time control running on the arm itself.
- **PCB design**: custom boards for motor control and sensing rather than a pile of dev kits.
- **CAD**: the mechanical design of the arm, its joints, and its end effector.

## Status

Early. Currently in the research and simulation phase, working through the roadmap layer by layer
with SLAM first.
